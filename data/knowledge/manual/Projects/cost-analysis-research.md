# Evaluative Research on Cost Analysis Tool

---

## Overview

**Timeline:** January 2023 – March 2023

Conducted evaluative research for a production cost analysis tool to assess usability, validate product value, and define future product direction. The research identified feature gaps, mapped current and future workflows, and helped secure continued project funding.

---

## My Role

**UX Designer**

- Sole UX representative on the project team

---

## Employer

**ExxonMobil**

---

## Problem

Reducing production costs was a major business objective, making visibility into production cost drivers and value erosion essential.

Development of a production cost analysis tool began in 2021. However, changes in Product Owners and Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) caused the project team to lose alignment on:

- Whether the tool addressed real user needs
- The product's long-term vision
- Future development priorities

Two months before release, I joined the project to evaluate usability and identify gaps between the existing solution and user expectations.

---

## Solution

I began by interviewing Product Owners and SMEs to understand their vision for the product.

These interviews revealed significant differences between stakeholder expectations and the project's current direction.

To evaluate the product, I conducted usability testing to determine:

- Overall usability
- Whether current functionality met user needs
- Missing capabilities
- Potential business value
- Desired user access levels

Based on the findings, I:

- Recommended improvements to access control
- Identified usability issues
- Prioritized feature enhancements
- Proposed new features
- Mapped current and future workflows
- Facilitated prioritization of functional pain points with SMEs

The research helped the team:

- Populate the product backlog
- Reconnect with users
- Align around product value
- Gain confidence to continue development

---

# Background

The production cost analysis tool calculates operational costs using three primary calculation models:

- Production calculations
- Mined volume calculations
- Cost calculations

At the time I joined, only the production and cost calculation logic had been implemented.

Development had paused due to resource limitations.

Although I was initially asked to improve the interface, it quickly became clear that the project also lacked a solid understanding of its target users and their workflows.

---

# Step 1 — Stakeholder Interviews

## Goal

Because the project's SMEs were relatively new to both the product and the problem space, I began with stakeholder interviews to better understand:

- Project history
- Business goals
- User workflows
- Product vision
- Success criteria
- Target user groups

I interviewed **six stakeholders**.

---

## Findings

The interviews uncovered several important alignment gaps that ultimately shaped the research strategy.

I also documented stakeholders' expectations around user access levels and permissions.

---

# Step 2 — Heuristic Evaluation

Before testing with users, I conducted a heuristic evaluation.

This served two purposes:

1. Assess the current usability of the application.
2. Become familiar with the product before facilitating usability studies.

The evaluation also helped establish credibility with stakeholders by demonstrating immediate, actionable findings.

The early usability issues I identified ultimately convinced the team to support formal usability testing.

---

# Research Objectives

Working with stakeholders, we aligned on four primary research objectives.

## 1. Evaluate Usability

Assess the usability of the MVP.

---

## 2. Validate Value Drivers

Determine whether the product addressed users' primary needs.

---

## 3. Assess Product Value

Understand whether users found the application useful in their daily work.

---

## 4. Define Access Control

Identify the appropriate permission model from users' perspectives.

---

# Step 3 — Usability Testing & Follow-Up Interviews

The project's primary user groups consisted of **Role A** and **Role B**, distributed across eight business teams.

Given project constraints, I recruited a representative sample consisting of:

- 2 participants from Role A
- 4 participants from Role B
- 1 newly assigned SME

When necessary, I conducted follow-up interviews to better understand workflows and gather additional requirements.

---

## Research Outcomes

### SUS Score

I measured the application's usability using the **System Usability Scale (SUS)**, providing stakeholders with an objective benchmark for evaluating the product.

---

### Usability Issues

Testing uncovered:

- Task failures
- Sources of redundant work
- Interface usability issues
- Bugs
- Workflow friction

---

### Feature Requests

Participants identified:

- Feature enhancements that would simplify daily work
- New capabilities that would significantly increase product value

---

### Access Model

I also documented users' preferred access permissions, which the project team later used to redesign the application's permission model.

---

### User Understanding

Using insights gathered throughout the research, I documented:

- Primary user characteristics
- User motivations
- Current ("as-is") workflows
- Future ("to-be") workflows
- The product's current and future value

The workflow maps highlighted exactly where the tool supported—or could better support—user tasks.

---

# Outcomes

The research established a clear direction for the project's next phase.

Key outcomes included:

- Clarified target user groups, their needs, and their workflows
- Delivered a prioritized backlog of actionable improvements
- Answered long-standing stakeholder questions about product value and future direction
- Demonstrated both the current value and long-term potential of the product, helping secure continued funding
- Established a repeatable framework for collecting, organizing, and prioritizing user requirements
- Reopened communication channels between the project team and its primary users
