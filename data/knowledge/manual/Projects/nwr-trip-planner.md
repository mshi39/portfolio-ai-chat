# Nemacolin Woodlands Resort Trip Planner

**Academic Project**

**Tags:** Product Design · UX Research · Service Design · Machine Learning · Hospitality

---

## Overview

**Timeline:** January 2019 – July 2019

Designed a machine learning–powered trip planning experience that helps prospective guests discover the unique value of Nemacolin Woodlands Resort before arrival, while providing personalized recommendations based on trip motivations and collecting behavioral data to continuously improve recommendations.

## Client

**Nemacolin Woodlands Resort**

---

## Problem

Nemacolin Woodlands Resort is a luxury five-star destination offering more than **80 activities** and highly personalized guest services.

Despite these offerings, booking conversions were below expectations.

Leadership wanted to:

- Increase bookings
- Better communicate the resort's unique value
- Encourage guests to reserve activities before arrival
- Better understand guest decision-making

---

## Solution

We designed a personalized trip planner that:

- Helps guests explore the resort through guided recommendations
- Personalizes activities based on trip motivations
- Collects decision-making data to improve future recommendations
- Modernizes the resort's digital experience while preserving its luxury brand

Machine learning continuously improves recommendations by learning from guest booking behaviors.

# Research Phase

## Background Research

Before designing solutions, I planned a series of research activities to answer three questions:

- What makes Nemacolin unique?
- Where are guests struggling?
- What trends are shaping hospitality and travel technology?

### Research Activities

- Interviewed three previous guests
- Evaluated the reservation experience by booking stays ourselves
- Conducted competitive and market analysis

---

## Key Findings

### Returning Guests Follow Routines

Repeat visitors rarely explored activities outside their existing habits.

---

### Staff Couldn't Promote Every Experience

With more than 70 activities available, reservation specialists struggled to remember every offering.

---

### Activities Were the Resort's Greatest Competitive Advantage

The breadth of experiences differentiated Nemacolin from competing luxury resorts.

---

### Opportunity Before Arrival

Very few hospitality products focused on helping guests during the planning stage before arriving on-site.

---

# Quantitative Analysis

Working alongside our lead developer, we analyzed three years of reservation data.

### Key Findings

- Younger guests (ages 35–45) spent more than older demographics.
- Dining, spa, and recreation generated the largest share of revenue.

---

## Target Audience

The project focused on:

- Ages 35–45
- Upper-middle-income households
- Families near the Pittsburgh area
- Children between ages 6–15

---

# Exploratory Research

To better understand why guests booked—or failed to book—we conducted a three-month research initiative.

Activities included:

- Contextual inquiry with resort staff
- Contextual inquiry with prospective guests

---

# Contextual Inquiry — Resort Staff

During a two-day onsite visit, I observed:

- Concierge
- Front desk
- Bell staff
- Restaurant management

## Key Findings

### Reservations Shape Guest Experience

Guests who reserved experiences before arrival enjoyed significantly better visits.

---

### Limited Understanding of Pre-Arrival Behavior

The resort had little visibility into how guests planned their trips before arriving.

---

# Contextual Inquiry — Prospective Guests

Participants completed a realistic booking exercise before participating in follow-up interviews.

## Key Discovery

Guests were not primarily differentiated by demographics.

Instead, they were distinguished by **trip motivation**.

Motivation influenced:

- Planning behavior
- Information needs
- Perceived value
- Booking decisions

This became the foundation of our personalization strategy.

---

## Additional Findings

Factors influencing booking decisions included:

- Clear descriptions of hotel and room value
- Easy access to information
- Recommendations from trusted friends and family

---

# Machine Learning Analysis

Using reservation history, CRM data, and guest feedback, our team identified variables most predictive of hotel selection.

Important variables included:

- Reason for travel
- Season
- Month
- Day of week
- Number of adults
- Number of children
- Number of rooms

The resulting decision tree achieved approximately **43% prediction accuracy**.

# Design Process

## Iteration 1 — Reservation Approaches

We explored two booking experiences.

### Option 1

Guests build their itinerary while booking.

### Option 2

Guests receive a recommended itinerary during booking.

Testing revealed users preferred greater flexibility, leading us to continue with **Option 1**.

---

## Key Learnings

Users wanted:

- More flexibility
- Better affordances
- Richer activity information
- Clearer recommendations

The research also validated our mobile-first design approach.

---

# Iteration 2 — Flexible Itinerary Planning

We refined the experience by:

- Providing richer activity information
- Improving itinerary management
- Organizing recommendations by day

### Testing Findings

We identified additional improvements:

- Better explanation of the planner's purpose
- Stronger urgency around reservations
- More compelling presentation of resort offerings
- Ability to save favorite recommendations

---

# Iteration 3 — Guided Planning

To improve navigation, we redesigned the planner as a guided step-by-step journey.

### Research Questions

- Did users understand the tool?
- Did the workflow match expectations?
- Did users trust recommendations?
- Were next steps clear?

Testing confirmed the redesigned flow was intuitive.

However, new edge cases emerged, including:

- Returning to saved itineraries
- Handling scheduling conflicts
- Booking rooms without activities

We also began exploring automatic itinerary generation.

---

# Iteration 4 — Refinement

The final iteration focused on:

- Supporting secondary use cases
- Evaluating AI-generated itineraries
- Refining visual design

The design team explored **63 visual concepts** before selecting the final interface.

---

## User Feedback

Participants described the final experience as:

- Professional
- Modern
- Efficient
- Organized
- Helpful
- Clean
- Inviting
- Easy to use

Users showed no strong preference between manually building itineraries and automatically generated ones, suggesting both approaches should be supported.

---

# Activity Categorization

To improve recommendations, I led a card sorting exercise to categorize over **70 resort activities**.

Categories included:

- Relaxation
- Romance
- Family
- Culture
- Wellness
- Adventure

These groupings became the foundation of the recommendation engine.

---

# Final Solution

The final product is a machine learning–powered trip planning assistant that guides guests through four steps.

## The Four-Step Journey

1. Trip Quiz
2. Personalized Recommendations
3. Itinerary Builder
4. Booking

The planner personalizes recommendations while collecting behavioral data that continuously improves future recommendations.

---

# Design Principles

The final experience was guided by ten principles:

1. Digitize white-glove hospitality
2. Design for conversion
3. Accommodate changing guest demand
4. Streamline planning
5. Match users' planning mental model
6. Set expectations through transparency
7. Balance structure with flexibility
8. Encourage booking through scarcity
9. Present information clearly
10. Reflect the Nemacolin brand

---

# Visual Design

The interface extended Nemacolin's existing visual identity by reusing its typography, colors, and luxury aesthetic while modernizing the overall digital experience.

---

# Recommendation Engine

Recommendations are powered by a machine learning decision tree trained on:

- Reservation history
- Guest feedback
- Call center interactions

Rather than functioning as a traditional recommender system, the model classifies guests into hotel and activity categories based on predicted preferences.

---

# Results

## Business Outcomes

### Higher Booking Intent

Before testing:

- 53% of traffic came from mobile
- Only 19% of digital transactions occurred on mobile

After five rounds of usability testing:

- **100% (25/25)** of participants reported they would likely book a stay using the planner.

---

### Earlier Reservations

Previously:

- Approximately 50% of guests reserved experiences before arrival.

After testing:

- **92%** reported they would book ahead.
- **42%** said they would reserve earlier than they normally would.

---

## Guest Experience

Testing demonstrated that the planner:

- Simplified trip planning
- Improved confidence during booking
- Enhanced perceptions of Nemacolin as a premium luxury destination
